# The Bell-Board at Pelham Square — story bible (PRIVATE)

## The house
No. 11 Pelham Square, Mayfair. Home of Edmund Vane (50), railway contractor, and his wife
Clara (32), married three years, one infant son. Two worlds:
- Family side: entrance hall (footman's chair), library, dining-room, morning-room,
  cloakroom; main stair to first floor (drawing-room, Vane's dressing-room) and second
  floor (nursery).
- Servants' side: the back stair (limewashed, runs basement to second floor) with a jib
  door into the library and a door into the servery, baize doors onto the first- and
  second-floor landings; basement passage, servants' hall (the bell-board), butler's
  pantry (speaking-tube mouthpieces), kitchen.

## The dinner (Watson is a guest)
Eight courses, six at table: Vane, Clara, Lionel Brand (55, Vane's partner, unmarried),
Captain Hugh Orme (Watson's comrade from Maiwand), Miss Letitia Orme (his sister), Watson.

## Truth
Clara Vane was born Clara Dunn. In 1879, aged 23, she was lady's maid to Mrs Ashcombe of
Lansdown Crescent, Bath (Brand's sister), and was tried at Bath Quarter Sessions for
stealing a diamond brooch. She was **acquitted**; she was innocent. She re-made herself as
a clergyman's daughter and companion, and met and married Vane.

This week Mrs Ashcombe recognised Clara and told her brother. Before dinner Brand told
Clara privately that he would tell Vane tonight: he would not be party to a deception.
After the port began he went to the library to write it to Vane.

Clara left the drawing-room at 9:55 "to look in at the nursery", went through the
first-floor baize door and down the back stair, and into the library by the jib door. She
begged him; he refused, and turned to the speaking tube to summon the butler to have her
shown out. She struck him on the back of the head with the library poker as he blew it.
She burned his letter in the grate (fragments survive), but missed the newspaper cutting
in his waistcoat pocket. She went back up the back stair past the first floor to the
second-floor baize door and the nursery, where the baby had woken and been crying alone,
and rang the nursery bell at 10:05 so that the nursemaid would find her there.

The tragedy: she killed to hide a trial for a crime she did not commit, from a husband who
says he would not have cared.

## Clue chain (the deduction)
1. **When:** whistle-plug hanging out of the library tube; Gaunt heard the whistle at 10:00
   (Ellen confirms). Brand died at 10:00.
2. **Not the front way:** James's chair commands the library door; nobody entered after
   Brand. Even at the front door at 10:00 he could see it.
3. **Alibis at 10:00:** Vane and Watson in the dining-room (Vane had been with Parkes till
   9:58). Orme in the cloakroom, which has one door and a barred window. Gaunt in the
   pantry with Ellen. James at the front door. Miss Orme playing without a break.
4. **The other way:** a jib door in the library wall onto the back stair.
5. **Who was on the back stair:** Parkes, caught out by the cigar-end, heard silk skirts
   going up just after 10:00, past the first floor. Every maid was at supper (Cook);
   Miss Orme was at the piano. The only lady unaccounted for is Clara.
6. **Physical proof:** the back stair is limewashed and rubs off; a chalky smear on Clara's
   dark blue silk. The main stair is papered.
7. **The nursery:** Annie found Clara breathless, the baby frantic. Clara claims she went
   up the main stair at 9:55 and sat with the baby for ten minutes.
8. **Motive:** letter fragments in the grate; the cutting in Brand's waistcoat (Clara Dunn,
   23, lady's maid, Bath, 1879, acquitted); Vane says his wife was née Dunn, of Somerset,
   a companion at Bath, now two-and-thirty.

Red herrings: Orme's IOU for £600 and the dinner-table words; Vane's partnership being
dissolved (draft deed in the desk); Parkes's lie about coming straight down.

## Final puzzle (accuse)
who = Clara; route = the jib door from the back stair; time = ten o'clock; why = to keep a
secret of her past. Wrong endings for Orme, Vane, Miss Orme and each servant, plus a
default for "right name, wrong reasoning".
