# The Medium's Last Sitting — story bible (PRIVATE: spoilers)

Year moved from 1892 to **1890** (Holmes is "dead" May 1891 to 1894). Thursday 4 December
1890. Watson is married and in practice; Holmes wires him to come to St John's Wood.
The sitting was on Wednesday night, the 3rd. Twist: **none** (the user asked that the case
after Two Clocks have no misdirection twist). House style: obvious culprit, hard proof.

## The house

Laurel Lodge, Clifton Hill, St John's Wood: Madame Peake's house and séance rooms.
Ground floor: hall, front drawing-room (waiting room), dining-room, morning-room (french
window to the garden), library, office (Gale's), and the back drawing-room, the séance
room, with the curtained alcove ("the cabinet") behind the medium's chair. Kitchen below.
First floor: Madame's bedroom, Gale's room.

## The truth

**Victim:** Mrs Rosamund Peake, "Madame Peake", 44, medium; a widow. Spirit control
"Little Wren", a child. A fraud: in the dark her right hand is always free, because her
right-hand neighbour is always her confederate, Gale, who swears he holds it.

**Culprit:** Ferdinand Gale, 38, her secretary and manager; once assistant to "Signor
Volante", a music-hall conjuror who exposed spirit tricks on the stage. He taught her
the business and has been robbing her for two years (fees kept back from the bank).
She found it out, and was to marry Sir Walter Brayfield in January, give up public
sittings, and then prosecute Gale. They quarrelled on Wednesday afternoon (Effie heard:
"You'll answer for every penny of it to a magistrate, Ferdinand, the day after I'm
married"). She drafted a letter to her solicitor.

**Method.** The circle of eight, hands joined, at a round table five feet across. Dr Rook
of the psychical-research society sealed the door with paper slips and wax at 9:00 and
screwed the shutters; Effie sat outside the door. Rook took Madame's LEFT hand. Gale, as
always, took her RIGHT, which means he held nothing: her right hand was free to work
the luminous trumpet and a telescopic reaching rod with a little stuffed kid glove on
its tip (the "touches" on Sir Walter's cheek, Danby's hand, the Colonel's crown, all
far beyond arm's reach). So Gale's LEFT hand was free all evening, with no trick needed
on anyone. Mrs Quill held his right hand, truly, the whole time.

Before the sitting, Gale went back alone to the hall "for the key of the musical box"
and took one of the two hatpins from Miss Brayfield's hat on the hall-stand (jet head
carved as a scarab beetle). At about 9:40, as the musical box ran down, he drove the
pin into Madame's heart from her right side, with his free left hand. She cried out
once (taken for Little Wren coming) and dropped the trumpet. He pushed the pin up into
the underside of the table-top, under the baize fringe at his own place, and went on
holding Mrs Quill. He told the circle Madame was in deep trance and must not be woken
until 10:10, when Rook called for the lamp. Gale at once went up to his room "for
brandy for Sir Walter", changed his blood-spotted cuffs (detachable) and later, after
the police went, burnt the stained one in his grate. It did not burn through.

The police searched every sitter's person before letting them go, found nothing, and
did not find the pin (it is under the table, not on anyone).

## Clue graph

1. The wound: thin round puncture under the left breast; track runs from her right
   toward her left and backward: struck from her right side, close. Surgeon: thin blade
   about eight inches; death almost at once. Weapon not found; all sitters searched.
2. Her hands: wedding ring on the left; the fingertips of the right glow faintly in the
   dim alcove (luminous paint, as on the trumpet).
3. Reaching rod in a hidden pocket of her skirt; luminous paint on its grip.
4. Touches reached places 4, 5, 6; the table is five feet across.
5. Rook held her left hand all evening (ring under his thumb). So her right was free.
   Gale swore he held her right. So Gale held nothing with his left.
6. Hatpin under the table at place 8 (her right), blood on it; Cecily's; Effie saw Gale
   go back to the hall alone before the sitting.
7. Gale went upstairs as soon as the lamp came; cook: his fire lit after ten, he never
   has one; scorched cuff with blood in his grate.
8. Motive: quarrel; letter to solicitor; pass-book vs. sittings book fees.
9. Background: playbill (he knows every trick).

## Red herrings

- Dr Rook: on her left, within reach, and she was suing him for libel (Peake v. Rook).
  Cleared: the wound came from her right; his left was held by Cecily; his right held
  her left, which never moved.
- Miss Brayfield: hated her, the weapon is her hatpin. Cleared: place 3, both hands held
  (Rook, Fane), out of reach.
- Sir Walter: none really; opposite. Danby: paid ruinous fees. Quill, Fane: nothing.
- Effie: outside the door; seals unbroken.

## Final

Seating plan, then: who = Gale; free hand = Madame's own right was free for her tricks,
so the man who swore he held it had his left free; weapon = Miss Brayfield's hatpin;
why = she meant to prosecute him for robbing her.
