# The Two Clocks of Hatherleigh — story bible (PRIVATE: spoilers)

Year 1881, Wednesday 19 October. Holmes and Watson come down from Waterloo on the night
mail and the early branch train, arriving 10.40 a.m. (railway time). Early in their
partnership (Watson met Holmes in January 1881).

Hatherleigh here is a Devon market town on a (fictional) LSWR branch line opened 1879.
Longitude about 4° 4' W, so local mean time is 16 minutes behind Greenwich.

## The two clocks

- The Statutes (Definition of Time) Act, 2 August 1880, made Greenwich time the legal time
  of Great Britain. (Period texture: Curtis v. March, 1858, the Dorchester case where a man
  lost by arriving "late" by Greenwich time but early by local time.)
- **St John's church clock** keeps local time (16 min behind London), by the obstinate will
  of the vicar, the Rev. Oliver Squance. He winds it himself every Tuesday evening, with the
  sexton holding the lantern.
- **Railway (London) time** is kept by: the station (telegraph time signal at 10 a.m. daily),
  the bank (bills fall due by it), the King's Arms (since the station omnibus began in
  1879; painted notice "RAILWAY TIME KEPT HERE").
- **The schoolroom clock** is the crux. The schoolmaster, Lethbridge, keeps it on railway
  time and set it by the station on Saturday. But it is a church (National) school and the
  vicar is chairman of the managers: at the managers' meeting on Monday evening (17th) in
  the schoolroom, he found it "on London time again" and put it back to church time with his
  own hands. Minuted. Present: Squance (chair), Vodden, Gloyne (clerk). Lethbridge was not
  present (managers meet without the master) and never noticed: Tuesday he did not compare
  it, and the school is shut Wednesday for the tragedy. It is still 16 min slow by railway.
- Watson's own watch was set at Waterloo: railway time. Comparing it with each clock in
  game shows each clock's standard (relative only, never an absolute reading).

## The truth

**Victim:** Josiah Gloyne, 61, attorney, clerk to the magistrates and clerk to the
Hatherleigh Market Trust; unmarried; lives at Fore House on the Square, office on the
ground floor. A church-time man by habit.

**Culprit:** Arthur Vodden, 45, agent of the Devon & Cornwall Bank's Hatherleigh branch,
treasurer of the Market Trust, people's churchwarden, school manager. He had taken £812 of
Trust money to cover his own speculations in mining shares. Gloyne found the deficiency
and wrote him: come at nine on Tuesday, last chance, or he would lay it before a special
meeting of trustees on Thursday (draft notice in his desk).

**Method:** Vodden saw the vicar put the school clock back on Monday. Before leaving he
fixed a game of chess with Lethbridge for Tuesday: "nine o'clock, by your clock,
Lethbridge." He planned to be at the schoolhouse a few minutes after the deed, by a clock
everyone believed kept railway time, so that he would seem to have been there at the
moment of the shot.

On Tuesday he locked the bank at 8.45 (railway), took the bank's revolver from the counter
drawer, went home, came to Gloyne's back garden gate through the churchyard with his
churchwarden's key, and was let in by Gloyne at the garden door. Talk failed; he shot
Gloyne at 9.16 railway (= nine by the church, which struck as he fired). He went out by
the garden door (leaving it unbolted), through the churchyard (prints in the wet path:
the rain stopped just before nine), out by the School Lane lych gate, which in his hurry
he left unlocked, and knocked at the schoolhouse at 9.05 by the schoolroom clock = 9.21
railway. Four minutes' brisk walk. He put the revolver back in the bank drawer on
Wednesday morning, uncleaned (one chamber fired).

Face-value order is quite different (shot before the train; Vodden at the school level with
the train; Luxton out after the shot), which is the trap.

## Suspects (each airtight by one clock or the other)

- **Edward Gloyne** (nephew, heir, in debt). Quarrelled with his uncle at 8.15 (heard by
  Mrs Bickle leaving for chapel). Uncle turned him out because "someone was coming at nine
  on business". Left as the church struck the three-quarters, ran, caught the 9.05 moving
  (porter). Face value: 20 unexplained minutes. True: 4 minutes running. Innocent. Came
  back on the first train today.
- **William Luxton** of Week Farm. Gloyne was foreclosing his mortgage; Luxton threatened
  him in the market Tuesday afternoon. He is the vicar's churchwarden (Vodden is the
  people's), so he too has a churchyard key. Out of the tap 9.10–9.18 (inn = railway), i.e. during the
  shot. Cleared by the ostler, Huxtable: Luxton was in the stable with him, his mare cast in
  her stall, when they heard the shot. Innocent.
- **The Rev. Oliver Squance.** Gloyne had carried a vestry motion, to be confirmed Thursday,
  to put the church clock on Greenwich time. Has a key. In the clock chamber with the sexton
  at the shot. Innocent.
- **Mr Lethbridge**, schoolmaster: Vodden's alibi witness; honest; innocent.
- **Arthur Vodden**: alibi "at the schoolhouse at five past nine" taken by everyone as
  railway time, so apparently with Lethbridge at the shot (9.16). Guilty.

## Clue graph (needed for the solution)

1. The 16 minutes: stationmaster (longitude, the Act, time signal), Watson's watch vs
   church clock, porter's 9.16 for the shot vs the vicar's "nine".
2. Which standard each clock keeps: station, bank, inn = railway (testimony + watch);
   church = local; schoolroom = **church** (watch comparison in the schoolroom; minute book;
   vicar admits it when asked about the school clock).
3. The timeline cards (9 above).
4. Route: churchyard shortcut 4 minutes (Watson paces it); round by the Square nearer ten.
   School Lane lych gate found unlocked (sexton locked it at dusk); prints on the path
   from Gloyne's garden gate toward the lych gate, sharp, made after the rain stopped;
   garden door unbolted (Mrs Bickle). Keys: vicar, sexton, both churchwardens (Vodden,
   Luxton).
5. Motive: Gloyne's diary "Tues. 9 o'c. The Treasurer. Last chance."; draft notice of a
   special meeting re a deficiency of £812 in the Treasurer's account; Treasurer = Vodden
   (minute book list / Pring).
6. Premeditation: minute book (Vodden present when clock put back); Lethbridge: Vodden fixed
   the game "nine by your clock".
7. Weapon: bullet from the office wall, a small pistol ball (Sergeant Pascoe); bank
   revolver in the counter drawer, one chamber fired and foul (Pring: it was clean on
   Monday; Mr Vodden has the drawer key).

## Red herrings

Edward's quarrel and debts and his face-value gap; Luxton's threat, key and absence;
the vicar's quarrel over the clock and his key; Gloyne's own pistol in his desk drawer
(clean, loaded, unfired: he never reached it).

## Final puzzle

TIMELINE: the nine cards above in true order. Then:
- who: Vodden.
- how the alibi was made: the schoolroom clock had been put back to church time, and he
  knew it.
- route: through the churchyard with a churchwarden's key.
- why: Gloyne would expose his theft from the Market Trust.
Wrong timeline -> `muddle` ending. Wrong who -> per-suspect endings; right who but wrong
details -> `escape` ending (Vodden's counsel picks the case apart).
