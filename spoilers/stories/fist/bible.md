# The Operator's Fist — story bible (PRIVATE: spoilers)

1887. The murder: Wednesday 2 November 1887 (the first Wednesday of the month). Holmes and
Watson come down by the night mail on Thursday and play opens on Friday morning, 4 November.
Watson is still at Baker Street (before his marriage).

**Twist: YES.** This is the first misdirection case. Everything ordinary points at Julius
Tagg, the London speculator. The real culprit is Miss Loveday Nance, the village
telegraphist, who "was at her key two miles off, talking to the dead man" when he was
supposedly alive. The lynchpin, harder to find than the rest, is Pascoe's hidden store in a
dummy battery cell: his blackmail book and the 1885 wreck-night tape, which carries a sample
of her own sending with her habit of splitting the letter C. The same split C runs through
"Pascoe's" replies on Wednesday evening.

## Place

- **Polventon Head**: a fictional headland on the Lizard coast of Cornwall, with a Lloyd's
  signal station on it. Its telegraph call is PV. Ships homeward bound from the Atlantic
  make their landfall here and are reported to Lloyd's in London.
- **Porthkellis**: a fishing village a mile below the station. Its post office (PK) is a
  *sounder* office, read by ear, with no inker, and is open 8 to 8, closed for tea 6 to 7.
  The road from the station is the long way round; the cliff path is the short way (about
  twenty minutes' walk, less going down).
- **Helston** (HN), which had its new railway branch from May 1887.
- **The wire**: one line runs PV, PK, HN, then on to London. The station's inker is in the
  line and prints **every** signal on it, whoever sends it and from whichever end.
  Bolitho, the lineman, says so. A tape cannot tell you where a signal was keyed; only the
  fist can.

## People

- **Amos Pascoe**, 54, telegraph clerk at the station, ex-Electric Telegraph Company, and
  the victim. A quiet blackmailer: for years he has kept old tapes, against the rules, and
  read the district's private business off the line.
  - His fist: long dashes (4), wide letter gaps (4), word gap 8, clean letters.
- **Mrs Pascoe**, his wife. She went to her sister's in Helston on Wednesday (the 2:10
  train) and came back on Thursday.
  - Lately he had money and talked of "a little house in Helston".
  - These two years he spent more time in the battery room than the cells needed.
  - His pocket-book is missing.
- **Captain Elias Hext**, the Lloyd's agent in charge. He explains the overdue market and
  the *Corisande*.
  - He was at the lookout with Kitto from 6:15 to 7:45, watching for her lights, because
    Lloyd's had wired at noon to keep a sharp lookout.
  - Pascoe was "most particular" about destroying tapes after three months, by the rules.
- **Jory Kitto**, 22, signalman and lookout, who is learning telegraphy. He has a practice
  key and a little practice inker in the lookout hut.
  - He trims and fills the hand-lamps at 6:00 every evening. Pascoe's bull's-eye burns
    about three hours on a filling, and Pascoe lights it only to go out of doors.
  - He saw no vessel all evening, and certainly nothing at 8:50.
  - At 9:40 he found the instrument room dark, cold and unlocked, with the stove out.
    Pascoe always locked it when he stepped out. Kitto found the body at the stile with his
    own lantern; Pascoe's lamp lay beside it, burnt dry and cold.
  - On Monday Tagg showed off on the practice set; that tape is Tagg's sample.
  - Pascoe also had a small hold over Kitto (he once slept on watch), shown as "J.K." in the
    book. That is noise, and Kitto's alibi is good.
- **Inspector Trevail**, of the county constabulary at Helston, is sure of Tagg. He keeps
  the body and Pascoe's effects in the flag store. He has the surgeon's estimate (died
  between six and eight o'clock), Miss Nance's account of the 7:15 to 7:40 conversation,
  Tagg's boot-prints on the instrument-room step, and Hosken's word that Tagg was out from
  8:30 to 9:30.
- **Mr Bolitho**, the Post Office lineman, testing the line on the lane. He explains:
  - the single wire (above);
  - fists ("some split their C's so they read like two N's");
  - that PK is a sounder office;
  - that the line tested sound all Wednesday.
- **Julius Tagg**, an "outside broker" in the overdue market, of Leete & Tagg, Birchin Lane,
  staying at the Fisherman's Arms. He was once a Company telegraph clerk.
  - His fist: clipped, with short dashes (2), letter gap 2, word gap 4.
  - His partner Leete learnt privately, through an agent at Brest, that wreckage bearing the
    *Corisande*'s name had been picked up off Ushant. The plan: send a false "passed
    Polventon, all well" report, so that the reinsurance rate falls from 60 guineas per cent
    to almost nothing, then buy cover cheaply before the loss becomes known.
  - He bribed Pascoe £20 on Wednesday afternoon (Kitto saw him come and go about 3:00). His
    note in Pascoe's waistcoat reads "Be up at the lookout at nine and stay till the half
    hour. The other twenty when it is through. J.T."
  - He walked up the road at 8:30 and found the instrument room dark, cold and empty, as
    arranged (he thought). He lit the desk lamp, sent the message at 9:05, put out the lamp
    and went back to the inn by 9:30, muddy, and called for brandy.
  - He lies at first, then confesses to the message when shown his note. He never saw
    Pascoe.
- **Mr Hosken**, landlord of the Fisherman's Arms. Tagg dined in the coffee-room from 6 to
  8:30 with the commercial gentleman and the curate, so he has an alibi for 6:40. He was
  out from 8:30 to 9:30.
- **Miss Loveday Nance**, 26, telegraphist at Porthkellis post office. She is to marry Will
  Rowse in December, and she is helpful, gentle and grieved.
  - Her fist: dashes 3, letter gap 3, word gap 6, and she **splits C** (she does not know
    it).
  - When she imitates Pascoe she lengthens dashes to 4 and gaps to 4 (with an occasional
    slip back to 3), but still splits her C's.
- **Mrs Nance**, postmistress, her mother.
  - She was at the Wednesday class meeting at chapel from 6:30 to 7:30.
  - She came in at 7:35 and saw Loveday at the key "talking to Mr Pascoe".
  - The office is closed 6 to 7 for tea; Loveday "had her tea upstairs alone".
- **Will Rowse**, coxswain of the Porthkellis lifeboat. His brother Dick drowned when the
  schooner *Gleaner* of Porthkellis went ashore on Polventon Ledges in November 1885.
  - The lifeboat was launched seventy minutes late. The Board of Trade inquiry found "the
    line interrupted by the gale", on Miss Nance's evidence.
  - He had lifeboat drill from 6 to 8 on Wednesday, so he has an alibi.
  - He says Loveday "has not been herself this year".

## The truth

In November 1885, on the *Gleaner* night, Loveday was on night watch at PK during the gale
and fell asleep upstairs; the night bell didn't wake her. Pascoe called at 11:02 and again.
At 11:40, Helston called PK too, which proves the line was working. She answered at 12:12:
"SORRY CALLED AWAY CALLING COXSWAIN NOW". At the inquiry she said the line had been down.
Pascoe kept that tape, with his pencilled times, and has blackmailed her since December
1885: ten shillings on the first Wednesday of every month, paid at the stile on the cliff
path. On 1 November he wrote in his book: "L.N. a sovereign from Dec., or W.R. shall have
the tape." He would tell her fiancé.

She chose the first Wednesday because she had read Tagg's telegrams to Leete as they passed
through her office ("WILL WIRE NEWS FROM HEAD NINE WEDNESDAY"). She knew a London man would
be at the station at nine: a ready-made murderer.

Timeline, Wednesday 2 Nov:
- 2:10, Mrs Pascoe leaves for Helston. 3:00, Tagg pays Pascoe £20.
- 6:00, Kitto fills the lamps; the PK office closes for tea.
- 6:15, Hext and Kitto go up to the lookout. Loveday slips out of the back of the post office
  and up the cliff path.
- 6:30, Pascoe sends the evening report (passage A, pencilled 6:30). He has pencilled the
  time against every message all day, and there is nothing after this one.
- About 6:35, Pascoe lights his bull's-eye and goes out of the yard gate to the stile. He
  leaves the room unlocked (for Tagg later, and he means to be back).
- About 6:40, at the stile, she strikes him on the back of the head with a sea-stone from her
  bag, as he turns to look at the coin. She takes his pocket-book, looking for the tape; it
  isn't there. She leaves the £20 in his waistcoat (she is no thief) and throws the stone
  over the cliff. The lamp is left burning beside him.
- About 7:05, she is back and opens the office (late). The stove in the instrument room goes
  out unattended; Tagg finds it cold at nine.
- From 7:15 to 7:41 she keys both sides of a "conversation" from PK. The station inker
  prints it all:
  - B, her own fist: "PV PV PK ARE YOU THERE".
  - C, as Pascoe: "QUIET HERE EXPECT LONDON MAN AT NINE" (split C).
  - D, her own: "WHAT MAN".
  - E, as Pascoe: "CORISANDE MAN DONT CARE FOR HIM" (split C twice).
  - F, her own: "GOOD NIGHT".
  - G, as Pascoe: "GN". This one has no C, so its sender has to be inferred.
- 7:35, her mother comes in and sees her at the key.
- 8:30 to 9:30, Tagg goes up by the road. At 9:05 he sends H: "CORISANDE PASSED EAST ALL
  WELL PV" (Tagg's fist).
- 9:40, Kitto comes down, finds the room dark and the stove cold, searches, and finds the
  body at the stile.

The police surgeon (Thursday, small hours) puts death between six and eight. The police
reason that he was alive at 7:41 by the wire, so he was killed between 7:41 and 9:05, and
Tagg was out from 8:30. The wire is everyone's proof of life. The lamp is the timing gap
the player must infer: it was lit at about 6:35, burns three hours, and was found dry at
9:40. Had he gone out after 7:41, it would still have been alight. The cold stove and the
missing pencil times after 6:30 support this.

## Tapes (passages)

The evening roll is on the station inker; the player takes it from the instrument room.

| call | PV PV PK ARE YOU THERE | nance | Nance |
| quiet | QUIET HERE EXPECT LONDON MAN AT NINE | as_pascoe | Nance |
| whatman | WHAT MAN | nance | Nance |
| corman | CORISANDE MAN DONT CARE FOR HIM | as_pascoe | Nance |
| night | GOOD NIGHT | nance | Nance |
| gn | GN | as_pascoe | Nance (inferred) |
| false | CORISANDE PASSED EAST ALL WELL PV | tagg | Tagg |

Samples with known senders:
- Practice tape (in the lookout): Kitto's exercise "NOW IS THE TIME" (kitto), and Tagg's
  "COMPLIMENTS OF THROGMORTON STREET" (tagg). It is dealt once Kitto tells whose is whose.
- The wreck tape (in the dummy cell):
  - Pascoe's "PK PK URGENT SCHOONER ASHORE LEDGES CALL LIFEBOAT", pencilled 11:02.
  - The 12:12 reply, pencilled "PK" by Pascoe: "PV PK SORRY CALLED AWAY CALLING COXSWAIN
    NOW", in her natural fist (split C). Known sender: Miss Nance, the only clerk at PK
    since 1883 (Mrs Nance says so). Decoding it gives the clue `wreck_reply`, which unlocks
    the motive answer.

Answer: report goes to Pascoe, false to Tagg, and all the rest to Nance.

## Misdirection (what points at Tagg)

- Motive: the false report is worth thousands to him, and Pascoe could have betrayed it.
- He was there at nine, by his boot-prints and the landlord's evidence. His fist matches H.
- "Pascoe's" own words on the wire: "expect London man at nine, don't care for him."
- His note in the dead man's pocket reads like a lure.
- His lies.

His innocence: the note and the £20 show Pascoe was paid to stand aside, so Tagg had no
need to kill him. Tagg's alibi covers 6:40. He describes the room as cold and dark at nine.

## What turns it

- Decoding C and E: "Pascoe's" C's are split ("NNORISANDE"), but his genuine C's (A, and
  the wreck call) are clean.
- Bolitho's lesson: a tape can't tell which end keyed a signal.
- The lamp: dead before 7:15.
- **Lynchpin: the dummy cell.** The battery room's description notes "one cell at the end
  of the bottom shelf has no wire to its terminals". It holds the book (L.N. payments on
  first Wednesdays, the 1 Nov threat, J.K., and J.T. £20) and the wreck tape (her known
  sample, split C, and the motive). Rowse's story and the lifeboat board give the inquiry's
  finding, which the tape shows to be false.
