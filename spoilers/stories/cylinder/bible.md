# The Wax Cylinder Will (deduction case 1), 1894: PRIVATE STORY BIBLE

Twist: **no**. Obvious culprit (the beneficiary), hard proof. (Recorded in `spoilers/twists.md`.)

Difficulty: a step above the Palace. The final puzzle (`cylinder`) needs the player to
work out *why* parts of one cylinder sound true at two different speeds, and to trace
each part to its source by speed and background, where words alone match two sources.

## The facts

- **Cornelius Vane**, 71, inventor (phonograph recorder diaphragms; patents being sold to
  the Columbian Phonograph Syndicate of New York). The Larches, Highgate. Cataracts:
  could not read or write since Christmas. Angina. Died in his sleep about 4 a.m. on
  **Monday 7 May 1894**, naturally (Dr Lammas). He always struck his tuning fork,
  **A 435** (French pitch), before speaking into a phonograph. His voice: about 110.
- **Felix Orme**, 30, his assistant for three years. Lodges at 14 Bartholomew Terrace,
  Kentish Town (Mrs Prout), though living at the Larches since Vane fell ill in April.
  A short dry double cough since the winter. **The culprit.**
- **The fraud.** On **Wednesday 2 May** Orme brought Vane papers "for the American
  assignment", read them aloud (Vane could not see), and Vane signed, with Mrs Dunn and
  Bates witnessing. One of them was a new will leaving everything to Orme. Vane's old
  will (1889, Tulliver) left all to Clara and Edmund equally. The Syndicate wrote on
  5 May still asking for the signed assignment.
- The children contest the new will (want of knowledge and approval). On **Friday 11
  May** Orme produced a cylinder, "recorded by Mr Vane in his study on the afternoon of
  2 May, after signing, on the new machine at 160, with me by", to prove Vane knew.
- **How it was made.** On **Tuesday 8 May** Orme took Vane's old study phonograph
  (governor seized since March: runs at **120** only) "to mend", by cab, to his lodgings.
  He took four of Vane's cylinders from the study cabinet. On the night of **Wednesday
  9 May** (rain from 9 p.m. to 2 a.m.), about 11 p.m., he played fragments of them on the
  old machine at 120, horn lashed to the horn of his own phonograph (governor set at
  **150**), recording a new cylinder. Each fragment's start and stop leaves a click.
  St Cuthbert's cracked tenor (C, 262) struck eleven; a train whistled; rain on the
  skylight; Orme coughed. Mrs Prout heard "the old gentleman's voice" through the floor.

## Chains

1. **Fork and voice**: Vane's fork is 435 (Clara; the fork on the desk). Each source plays
   true where its fork reads 435: S1, S3, S5 at 160; S2, S4 at 120.
2. **Two speeds on one cylinder**: at 150 the background and parts 2, 5, 6 are natural,
   parts 1, 3, 4, 7 low; at 200 the fork reads 435 and parts 1, 3, 4, 7 are natural. Nothing
   is true at 160, the speed Orme swears to. So it is a copy, made in pieces.
3. **Sources**: words match two sources for parts 1, 3, 4, 5; speed (and the sounds under
   each part) settle which.
4. **Where**: the bell at 150 is 262, cracked: St Cuthbert's, Kentish Town (verger, bell
   board), not St Jude's, Highgate (E flat, 311: Clara). Orme's room: two machines, one
   governor seized at 120, his own set at 150, horns lashed; the four source cylinders in
   a hatbox; the cabinet's empty slots and Vane's catalogue.
5. **When**: eleven strokes at night, rain. Bates's rain book: rain only on the nights of
   the 3rd and the 9th (dry on the 2nd). Mrs Dunn: Orme sat up at the Larches every night
   from the 3rd until the death, and took the old machine away on the 8th. So the 9th:
   two days after Vane died. Mrs Prout: one rainy night last week, the old gentleman's
   voice, faster and slower, while St Cuthbert's struck eleven.
6. **What he signed**: Dunn and Bates: "the American assignment", read aloud by Orme;
   Lammas: Vane blind since Christmas; the Syndicate's letter of 5 May: assignment still
   not received. The will label is in Orme's hand (Clara).
7. **Death natural**: Lammas (angina, expected; no digitalis signs), the medicine book
   (drops accounted for). Edmund's poison story is a red herring; so are his debts.

## Final answers

Sources: 1 S1, 2 S4, 3 S3, 4 S1, 5 S2, 6 S2, 7 S3.
what = pieced; who = orme; where = kentish; when = ninth; signed = assignment; death = natural.
